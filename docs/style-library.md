# Style library

The techniques behind the 8 Style Packs (`src/styles/packs/`). They were noted by watching seven public YouTube Shorts frame by frame (one frame per second, from YouTube's own preview thumbnails). Only editing techniques are recorded here: layout, type, colour, motion and caption habits. No footage, text, logos, people or audio were copied, and no reference file is stored in this repo.

References: `iAN5ZZOWxEU`, `ZIOS20gwsGc`, `digXr16EWrc`, `KvXsOsbBS30`, `uIIApzRn14A`, `bMjzIr8OlQE`, plus `khYDC9qhOLo` for Agent Mode.

## What the references share
- **Captions are small.** 1 to 3 words, about 50 to 60 px on a 1080 px frame, in the lower middle (60 to 66% from the top), not huge karaoke blocks.
- **Most frames are not AI pictures.** Designed graphics, UI cards, charts, quote cards and pill lists alternate with real footage.
- **One look per video.** One background colour family, one heading font, one accent. The accent marks the payoff word or line.
- **Mono labels as texture.** Tiny monospace annotations (figure numbers, step numbers) make a frame look "designed".
- **Shot length.** 1.5 to 3.5 s; a new graphic usually lands on a spoken keyword.
- **Picture in picture.** The speaker shrinks into a small rounded card while a graphic takes the top of the frame.

## Agent Mode (talking-head edit), from `khYDC9qhOLo` and `uIIApzRn14A`
- Base layer is the speaker, never cut. Captions are a small cyan box in the lower middle.
- When the speaker names a concept, a dark UI diagram takes the full frame and builds step by step (pills appear one per spoken step), then cuts back to the speaker.
- Logos or app cards pop in on names. A short italic accent word ("Same *Loop*") lands on the punchline.
- The speaker shrinks to a picture-in-picture card while a graphic explains.
